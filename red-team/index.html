Engineering assurance / Failure Lab

How can a governed AI workflow fail?

A malicious invoice note, a hidden command or an invented finding can look ordinary to a model. This lab connects each attack to the trust boundary it targets, the engineering control designed to stop it, and the failure that may still remain.

Untrusted inputBounded reasoningHuman authority

Threat landscape

What are we defending against?

The categories follow our red-team techniques and assurance arguments. A named control is a defence to challenge, not a promise of invulnerability.

01Input trust boundary

Direct & indirect prompt injection

Supplier notes or embedded document text attempt to become instructions for the assistant.

Control: Separate data from instructions; sanitise, delimit and limit what the model can do.

Remaining gap: Signature filtering misses unfamiliar paraphrases; the reviewer gate remains independent.

02Input normalisation

Obfuscation & encoding evasion

An attacker hides an instruction using spacing, look-alike characters, encodings or zero-width text.

Control: Normalise known variants and test beyond fixed strings; the optional semantic screen adds redactions when enabled.

Remaining gap: Normalisation is incomplete and the optional model screen is fallible and off by default.

03Human decision gate

Role spoofing & goal hijacking

Text pretends to be an administrator or reviewer and demands an unauthorised approval.

Control: Closed state transitions and typed reviewer decisions, not a text instruction, control authority.

Remaining gap: A correctly attributed human could still approve without scrutiny.

04Typed state validation

State & schema smuggling

A payload invents extra fields or permissions and tries to carry them to later workflow steps.

Control: Frozen typed state with extra='forbid' rejects unrecognised fields.

Remaining gap: Schema checking cannot establish that a permitted field is factually correct.

05Output filtering

Sensitive output leakage

A response is induced to reveal hidden instructions, secrets or internal references.

Control: Scrub known sensitive patterns and restrict permitted output and tool scopes.

Remaining gap: Unexpected encodings or novel leakage patterns still require adversarial testing.

06Claim verification

Fabricated evidence & consensus

Agents agree on a persuasive number or narrative that has no support in case records.

Control: Recompute case-bound findings and pass claims through deterministic verification before showing them.

Remaining gap: A true number may still be framed misleadingly; absent source evidence remains a blind spot.

AI-1 · Risk mechanism

Fabrication — a model proposes a finding the evidence does not support.

Exploratory method
Failure consequence

A reviewer approves or rejects on a number that was never in the submission, and the audit trail records it as a checked finding.

Defensive mechanism

Chain-of-Verification, twice. Before the Judge, each proposed finding's code, invoice identity and value must match recomputed evidence; after it, the numbers are re-derived and the sentence is REBUILT from them, so the proposal's own wording is discarded rather than trusted.

Residual limitation

A claim that is arithmetically true but misleading in its framing survives verification. CoVe checks derivation, not pertinence.
