Map · What is this use case?

Understand the context and possible harm

01 What decision or business task is being supported? What are the non-AI alternatives?

Answer: Describe the precise task, success criteria and available non-AI approaches. AI is justified only when its expected benefit outweighs its additional uncertainty, cost and risks. Explicit calculations and business rules should remain deterministic.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Compute quantity × contract rate in code. Consider bounded AI for interpreting an unusual supplier explanation; never use language-model confidence as payment authorization.

Evidence to collect

Problem statement, manual baseline, alternatives comparison, intended-use and excluded-use statements.

02 Who develops, supplies, deploys, operates and is affected by the system?

Answer: Separate provider/developer, deployer, owner, operator, reviewer and affected-party responsibilities. Include stakeholders who never interact with the model but bear the consequences of its output.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Accounts payable reviews invoices, procurement owns contract terms, suppliers face payment delays or challenges, and a named approver retains decision authority.

Evidence to collect

Stakeholder/affected-party map, accountable owners, operating roles and decision-rights matrix.

03 Which data, third parties, environments and foreseeable misuses are involved?

Answer: Identify authorized data sources, transformations, model suppliers, environments, interfaces and foreseeable misuse. Treat external documents as evidence to inspect, never as authoritative system instructions.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Trace invoice fields to approved purchase orders and effective-dated rate cards; test what happens when a supplier document contains an instruction to ignore the contract.

Evidence to collect

Source inventory, lineage and interface map, vendor dependencies, trust boundaries and misuse scenarios.

04 Where are the system's capability limits, knowledge limits and human authority boundaries?

Answer: Document known limitations and unsupported claims, define when users may rely on outputs, and reserve consequential decisions for authorized people. A missing or contradictory source must prompt abstention or escalation.

Supplier-invoice illustration

A model may recommend investigation of a disputed charge, but cannot approve a payment or invent a missing contract clause.

Evidence to collect

Capability/knowledge limitations, harm register, human oversight and escalation specification.

Measure · How do we know?

Evaluate the risks with evidence

01 Which failures, affected groups and harms are plausible? What are their severity and likelihood?

Answer: Turn mapped harms into explicit failure scenarios, characterize severity, likelihood and affected scope, and justify prioritization. Distinguish evidence from assumptions; no organization can measure an unspecified risk.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Unauthorized rate acceptance could cause an incorrect payment; a minor format warning has different consequences. Evaluate both rather than treating every error equally.

Evidence to collect

Risk-to-test traceability, harm assessment, severity/likelihood rationale and prioritized risk register.

02 What evidence supports accuracy, robustness, security, privacy and fairness claims?

Answer: Use independent reference answers and representative cases. Measure error rates, robustness, information security, privacy and fairness where relevant, including human usability. Report sample sizes and uncertainty.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Compare invoice totals and disputed rates against signed contracts, not against a second AI answer. Examine false acceptance, missed exceptions and cases requiring human correction.

Evidence to collect

Validated test cases, ground-truth sources, acceptance criteria, results, denominators and limitations.

03 How will third-party models, prompt injection, data quality and real-world operating conditions be tested?

Answer: Test vendor and data changes, adversarial inputs, prompt injection, conflicting or expired documents, integration failures and real operational conditions. Verify that safeguards actually contain failures.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Embed an instruction in invoice text to bypass the rate check; confirm it cannot override the trusted workflow or permit an unauthorized payment.

Evidence to collect

Adversarial/security test reports, integration tests, robustness and regression results, documented gaps.

04 What thresholds, denominators, monitoring signals and reassessment triggers are defined?

Answer: Specify risk-based thresholds before testing, with named evaluators, data sources and decision triggers. Monitor changes over time; 'not measured' is not a passed test or proof of no risk.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Define a maximum tolerable false-acceptance rate for disputed rates before a pilot; if evidence is insufficient, require manual review rather than publish a fabricated KPI.

Evidence to collect

Metrics and denominators, predefined release thresholds, monitoring plan, reassessment criteria and unresolved-risk record.

Manage · What action follows?

Treat risk and establish accountable decisions

01 What safeguards, human review, vendor obligations and operating instructions are required?

Answer: Link every significant risk to a selected treatment: mitigate, avoid, transfer where appropriate, or formally accept. Implement proportionate safeguards and verify that they work; contractual transfer does not erase your accountability.

Supplier-invoice illustration

Use independent contract validation and reviewer sign-off for exceptions; do not permit automated approval of uncertain charges.

Evidence to collect

Risk treatment decisions, control owners, technical and procedural safeguards, vendor obligations and verification results.

02 Who owns each residual risk, and who has authority to approve, defer or stop deployment?

Answer: Assign individual accountability for residual risk and explicit authority to approve, defer, override or suspend the use case. A model output is advice, not organizational approval.

Supplier-invoice illustration

The invoice reviewer may reject an AI recommendation; the governance owner can stop a release when a critical control has not passed testing.

Evidence to collect

Decision-rights/RACI record, risk acceptance, authorized release conditions, human override and escalation logs.

03 What incidents, drift or material changes trigger escalation and reassessment?

Answer: Set triggers for incidents, model drift, source updates, third-party changes and new uses. Escalate, contain, investigate, correct and revalidate rather than leaving the original approval unchanged forever.

Supplier-invoice illustration

A new rate-card source produces more unsupported charges; suspend automatic routing, review cases, correct the integration and retest before re-enabling.

Evidence to collect

Incident plan, monitoring signals, stop thresholds, change approvals, root-cause and retest records.

04 Where are decisions, exceptions, training and periodic review evidence recorded?

Answer: Use one controlled assessment record with the rationale, evidence references, exceptions, decision maker, training completion and scheduled reassessments. Ensure audit records reveal what was known at decision time.

Supplier-invoice illustration

For an invoice-review pilot, record test results, rejected AI proposals, reviewer decisions and the date of the next control review.

Evidence to collect

Signed decision record, issue tracker, audit events, training register, review log and retention controls.

What belongs in the accountable use-case record?

  1. Business purpose and non-AI alternatives
  2. Developers, deployers, users, affected groups and decision owner
  3. Intended/prohibited uses, contracts, data and system limits
  4. Risk descriptions, severity, likelihood and uncertainty
  5. Tests, acceptance thresholds, open defects and residual risk
  6. Authorized disposition, monitoring, incident response and next review trigger

Possible human dispositions: advance, advance with conditions, defer and escalate, or do not proceed. These are illustrative categories, not decisions made by this page.

Thirteen oversight and accountability policies — grouped and answered

A policy's primary function does not imply exclusive ownership. GOVERN establishes responsibilities across all functions, and each policy also connects to other stages.

POLICIES / GOVERN

GOVERN · Cross-cutting risk ownership, data stewardship, traceability and user competence.

Primary: Govern · Also Map · Measure · Manage

Risk management

Are risks evaluated, owned, treated and reconsidered throughout the life cycle?

Answer: Govern sets risk tolerance, owners and review cadence. Map identifies hazards, Measure evaluates their credibility and impact, and Manage chooses and tracks a treatment.

Example

Track unsupported contract-rate advice from discovery through test evidence, treatment and periodic review.

Evidence

Risk register, impact assessment, treatments and periodic review log

Primary: Govern · Also Map · Measure

Data governance

Are data rights, quality, minimization, privacy and retention responsibilities established?

Answer: Define data ownership, authorized purpose, access, quality, minimization, privacy protections and retention. Establish the applicable legal basis rather than assuming consent always applies.

Example

Only necessary invoice and contract fields reach the workflow; sensitive supplier information is access-controlled.

Evidence

Data inventory, lawful-use analysis, quality rules, access and retention controls

Primary: Govern · Also Map · Measure

Data lineage

Can the organization trace sources, transformations, labeling and training/evaluation boundaries?

Answer: Record data sources, versions, transformation steps and labels. Keep training, validation and test data appropriately separate when developing models; for vendor inference, trace real inputs and outputs.

Example

Trace a rate discrepancy from original document through parsing, signed rate card, validation and human decision.

Evidence

Provenance records, transformation history and train/validation/test separation

Primary: Govern · Also Manage

User training

Do operators understand permitted use, limitations, escalation and unsafe reliance?

Answer: Train each role on permitted use, limitations, how to verify outputs, when to override or report problems and how practices change over time.

Example

Accounts-payable staff learn that AI-generated contract interpretations cannot amend supplier rates.

Evidence

Role-based training, user instructions and refresh records

POLICIES / MAP

MAP · Define why the AI use case exists, who it may affect, and its data and third-party boundaries.

Primary: Map · Also Govern · Measure · Manage

Use case assessment

Is the application justified, feasible and aligned with organizational values and risk appetite?

Answer: Compare AI with ordinary software, define expected value and affected parties, categorize the use case under applicable organizational policy, and document why the choice is justified.

Example

Use deterministic arithmetic for invoice totals and consider bounded AI only for disputed explanations.

Evidence

Intake record, alternatives considered, initial classification and approval pathway

Primary: Map · Also Govern · Measure

Ethics by design

Whose interests, rights and foreseeable harms are considered before design decisions are locked in?

Answer: Identify affected individuals and groups before design decisions are fixed; examine fairness, autonomy, privacy and adverse outcomes, and record difficult tradeoffs for accountable review.

Example

Test whether uncommon supplier formats are escalated or rejected unfairly.

Evidence

Stakeholder and harm assessment, tradeoff rationale and ethics review

Primary: Map · Also Govern · Measure · Manage

Third-party risk

Are vendor components, contracts and inherited limitations understood?

Answer: Map supplier dependencies and data transfers, verify relevant vendor claims, document contractual security and change-notification duties, and plan mitigation or exit.

Example

Check hosted-model data handling, versions, failure modes and fallback before sharing invoices.

Evidence

Supplier due diligence, model/system documentation and contractual assurance requirements

Primary: Map · Also Govern · Measure

Model / system development and documentation

Are architecture, intended use, assumptions, dependencies and limitations documented?

Answer: Maintain architecture, interfaces, intended uses, assumptions, model/data provenance where available, limitations, evaluation results and versions. Declare missing vendor documentation honestly.

Example

Document invoice parser, deterministic rules, AI committee boundaries and the human approval gate.

Evidence

System description, model or system cards, versions and change records

POLICIES / MEASURE

MEASURE · Select and perform appropriate evaluations, documenting evidence and uncertainty.

Primary: Measure · Also Map · Manage

Interpretability

Can relevant users and overseers understand the system's outputs and limitations?

Answer: Provide explanations usable by the person responsible for the decision, and test whether those explanations faithfully reflect verified inputs. Fluent prose by itself is not evidence.

Example

Display the applicable contract clause and rate behind a flagged charge, not merely 'the AI thinks this is wrong'.

Evidence

Task-appropriate explanations, factor descriptions and explanation evaluation

Primary: Measure · Also Manage

Testing and validation

Is behavior validated against realistic tasks, affected populations and operating conditions?

Answer: Validate system behavior using representative conditions, independently checked reference results, known adversarial cases and documented uncertainties. Retest changed components.

Example

Check rates, totals, missing records and exceptions against approved source documents over varied invoice formats.

Evidence

Validation protocol, performance, robustness and fairness findings with limitations

Primary: Measure · Also Map

Testing types

Are evaluation methods and acceptance criteria chosen for the actual risk?

Answer: Derive test methods from mapped harms: functional correctness, adversarial robustness, security, privacy, safety and fairness or usability where relevant. Predefine criteria and limitations.

Example

Test arithmetic correctness, prompt-injection containment, stale contracts and unsupported AI claims separately.

Evidence

Test strategy covering accuracy, adversarial resilience, security and safety where relevant

POLICIES / MANAGE

MANAGE · Act on the results, protect human authority and respond to incidents or changes.

Primary: Manage · Also Govern · Measure

Deployment oversight

Are deployment gates, user notices, human oversight and fallback procedures defined?

Answer: Require evidence-based release gates, named authorization, human oversight, user instructions, monitoring and fallback or rollback. Do not infer deployment approval from a build passing.

Example

Launch advisory invoice flags only after validation; disputed payment approval stays with a reviewer.

Evidence

Release decision, operating procedures, monitoring and human-control plan

Primary: Manage · Also Govern · Map

Incident management

Can failures or harm be detected, reported, contained, investigated and escalated?

Answer: Establish accessible reporting, severity triage, containment, stakeholder notification, root-cause review and verified corrective action, including regulatory reporting where applicable.

Example

An unsupported rate recommendation triggers an incident record, reviewer escalation and regression test before restoration.

Evidence

Incident procedure, severity routing, root-cause review and corrective-action records

Worked example: disputed supplier rate

MAP: A supplier invoice claims a changed rate. The applicable signed contract, supplier impact, potential overpayment and human authority must be defined.

MEASURE: Validate the invoiced rate against an independent approved rate card and test stale documents, conflicting text and prompt injection. Record false-acceptance results.

MANAGE: Use deterministic comparisons, source-provenance checks and human approval for disputed charges; monitor overrides, incidents and contract changes.
