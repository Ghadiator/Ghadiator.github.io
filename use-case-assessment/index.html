01 · Three linked functions, with answers

MAP identifies · MEASURE evaluates · MANAGE acts

Start with the principle, then inspect each question's answer, supplier-invoice example and supporting evidence. GOVERN establishes ownership throughout. The functions are iterative—not a legal classification or a linear certification checklist.

FUNCTION 01 / 03

Map · Context, purpose & affected people

Is AI appropriate for this specific task?

What this function does: Document the setting before selecting a model. Separate what can be solved by ordinary software from what genuinely requires AI.

01What decision or business task is being supported? What are the non-AI alternatives?

Answer: Describe the precise task, success criteria and available non-AI approaches. AI is justified only when its expected benefit outweighs its additional uncertainty, cost and risks. Explicit calculations and business rules should remain deterministic.

Practical example

Compute quantity × contract rate in code. Consider bounded AI for interpreting an unusual supplier explanation; never use language-model confidence as payment authorization.

Evidence required

Problem statement, manual baseline, alternatives comparison, intended-use and excluded-use statements.

02Who develops, supplies, deploys, operates and is affected by the system?

Answer: Separate provider/developer, deployer, owner, operator, reviewer and affected-party responsibilities. Include stakeholders who never interact with the model but bear the consequences of its output.

Practical example

Accounts payable reviews invoices, procurement owns contract terms, suppliers face payment delays or challenges, and a named approver keeps the final say.

Evidence required

Stakeholder/affected-party map, accountable owners, operating roles and decision-rights matrix.

03Which data, third parties, environments and foreseeable misuses are involved?

Answer: Identify authorized data sources, transformations, model suppliers, environments, interfaces and foreseeable misuse. Treat external documents as evidence to inspect, never as authoritative system instructions.

Practical example

Trace invoice fields to approved purchase orders and effective-dated rate cards; test what happens when a supplier document contains an instruction to ignore the contract.

Evidence required

Source inventory, lineage and interface map, vendor dependencies, trust boundaries and misuse scenarios.

04Where are the system's capability limits, knowledge limits and human authority boundaries?

Answer: Document known limitations and unsupported claims, define when users may rely on outputs, and reserve consequential decisions for authorized people. A missing or contradictory source must prompt abstention or escalation.

Practical example

A model may recommend investigation of a disputed charge, but cannot approve a payment or invent a missing contract clause.

Evidence required

Capability/knowledge limitations, harm register, human oversight and escalation specification.

Function output: Intended-use statement · stakeholder and impact map · system boundaries · initial risk register

FUNCTION 02 / 03

Measure · Evaluation and evidence

How serious and credible are the risks?

What this function does: Choose tests that match the use case and population. Record results, limitations, uncertainty and who reviewed the evidence.

01Which failures, affected groups and harms are plausible? What are their severity and likelihood?

Answer: Turn mapped harms into explicit failure scenarios, characterize severity, likelihood and affected scope, and justify prioritization. Distinguish evidence from assumptions; no organization can measure an unspecified risk.

Practical example

Unauthorized rate acceptance could cause an incorrect payment; a minor format warning has different consequences. Evaluate both rather than treating every error equally.

Evidence required

Risk-to-test traceability, harm assessment, severity/likelihood rationale and prioritized risk register.

02What evidence supports accuracy, robustness, security, privacy and fairness claims?

Answer: Use independent reference answers and representative cases. Measure error rates, robustness, information security, privacy and fairness where relevant, including human usability. Report sample sizes and uncertainty.

Practical example

Compare invoice totals and disputed rates against signed contracts, not against a second AI answer. Examine false acceptance, missed exceptions and cases requiring human correction.

Evidence required

Validated test cases, ground-truth sources, acceptance criteria, results, denominators and limitations.

03How will third-party models, prompt injection, data quality and real-world operating conditions be tested?

Answer: Test vendor and data changes, adversarial inputs, prompt injection, conflicting or expired documents, integration failures and real operational conditions. Verify that safeguards actually contain failures.

Practical example

Embed an instruction in invoice text to bypass the rate check; confirm it cannot override the trusted workflow or permit an unauthorized payment.

Evidence required

Adversarial/security test reports, integration tests, robustness and regression results, documented gaps.

04What thresholds, denominators, monitoring signals and reassessment triggers are defined?

Answer: Specify risk-based thresholds before testing, with named evaluators, data sources and decision triggers. Monitor changes over time; 'not measured' is not a passed test or proof of no risk.

Practical example

Define a maximum tolerable false-acceptance rate for disputed rates before a pilot; if evidence is insufficient, require manual review rather than publish a fabricated KPI.

Evidence required

Metrics and denominators, predefined release thresholds, monitoring plan, reassessment criteria and unresolved-risk record.

Function output: Impact assessment · evaluation plan and test results · risk ratings with rationale · measurement limits

FUNCTION 03 / 03

Manage · Action, accountability & monitoring

Who may authorize the next step, under what controls?

What this function does: Choose whether to mitigate, avoid, transfer or formally accept risks, then establish explicit oversight and change control.

01What safeguards, human review, vendor obligations and operating instructions are required?

Answer: Link every significant risk to a selected treatment: mitigate, avoid, transfer where appropriate, or formally accept. Implement proportionate safeguards and verify that they work; contractual transfer does not erase your accountability.

Practical example

Use independent contract validation and reviewer sign-off for exceptions; do not permit automated approval of uncertain charges.

Evidence required

Risk treatment decisions, control owners, technical and procedural safeguards, vendor obligations and verification results.

02Who owns each residual risk, and who has authority to approve, defer or stop deployment?

Answer: Assign individual accountability for residual risk and explicit authority to approve, defer, override or suspend the use case. A model output is advice, not organizational approval.

Practical example

The invoice reviewer may reject an AI recommendation; the governance owner can stop a release when a critical control has not passed testing.

Evidence required

Decision-rights/RACI record, risk acceptance, authorized release conditions, human override and escalation logs.

03What incidents, drift or material changes trigger escalation and reassessment?

Answer: Set triggers for incidents, model drift, source updates, third-party changes and new uses. Escalate, contain, investigate, correct and revalidate rather than leaving the original approval unchanged forever.

Practical example

A new rate-card source produces more unsupported charges; suspend automatic routing, review cases, correct the integration and retest before re-enabling.

Evidence required

Incident plan, monitoring signals, stop thresholds, change approvals, root-cause and retest records.

04Where are decisions, exceptions, training and periodic review evidence recorded?

Answer: Use one controlled assessment record with the rationale, evidence references, exceptions, decision maker, training completion and scheduled reassessments. Ensure audit records reveal what was known at decision time.

Practical example

For an invoice-review pilot, record test results, rejected AI proposals, reviewer decisions and the date of the next control review.

Evidence required

Signed decision record, issue tracker, audit events, training register, review log and retention controls.

Function output: Risk treatment plan · named decision owners · conditional gate decision · monitoring and incident plan

02 · Accountable evidence

What an assessment record needs to make review possible

A use-case intake is more than a yes/no questionnaire. Every judgment should be linked to a reason, relevant evidence and a responsible person, including the choice not to use AI.

  1. 01

    Purpose and scope

    Decision supported, intended and prohibited uses, non-AI alternatives

  2. 02

    Actors and impacts

    Developer/deployer role, system owner, affected groups, intended users

  3. 03

    System and data

    Components, vendors, input sources, transformations, limits and provenance

  4. 04

    Risk and evaluation

    Harms, impact/likelihood rationale, methods, results and uncertainties

  5. 05

    Risk treatment

    Safeguards, residual risks, thresholds and contingency measures

  6. 06

    Human decision and review

    Named reviewer, evidence references, disposition and next review trigger

03 · Policies for oversight and accountability

All thirteen policy questions, grouped and answered

Four groups clarify primary ownership. Each card also names the other functions it supports—policies operate across the AI life cycle, not in isolated silos. Developer, deployer and vendor responsibilities may differ.

GOVERN · Organization-wide policies

Risk ownership, data stewardship, traceability and user competence apply throughout the other functions.

Govern · PrimaryAlso: Map · Measure · Manage

Risk management

Are risks evaluated, owned, treated and reconsidered throughout the life cycle?

Answer: Govern sets risk tolerance, owners and review cadence. Map identifies hazards, Measure evaluates their credibility and impact, and Manage chooses and tracks a treatment.

Practical example: Track unsupported contract-rate advice from discovery through test evidence, treatment and periodic review.

Evidence to collect: Risk register, impact assessment, treatments and periodic review log

Govern · PrimaryAlso: Map · Measure

Data governance

Are data rights, quality, minimization, privacy and retention responsibilities established?

Answer: Define data ownership, authorized purpose, access, quality, minimization, privacy protections and retention. Establish the applicable legal basis rather than assuming consent always applies.

Practical example: Only necessary invoice and contract fields reach the workflow; sensitive supplier information is access-controlled.

Evidence to collect: Data inventory, lawful-use analysis, quality rules, access and retention controls

Govern · PrimaryAlso: Map · Measure

Data lineage

Can the organization trace sources, transformations, labeling and training/evaluation boundaries?

Answer: Record data sources, versions, transformation steps and labels. Keep training, validation and test data appropriately separate when developing models; for vendor inference, trace real inputs and outputs.

Practical example: Trace a rate discrepancy from original document through parsing, signed rate card, validation and human decision.

Evidence to collect: Provenance records, transformation history and train/validation/test separation

Govern · PrimaryAlso: Manage

User training

Do operators understand permitted use, limitations, escalation and unsafe reliance?

Answer: Train each role on permitted use, limitations, how to verify outputs, when to override or report problems and how practices change over time.

Practical example: Accounts-payable staff learn that AI-generated contract interpretations cannot amend supplier rates.

Evidence to collect: Role-based training, user instructions and refresh records

MAP · Suitability, context and consequences

Identify business purpose, stakeholders, ethical impacts, external dependencies and intended system boundaries.

Map · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Measure · Manage

Use case assessment

Is the application justified, feasible and aligned with organizational values and risk appetite?

Answer: Compare AI with ordinary software, define expected value and affected parties, categorize the use case under applicable organizational policy, and document why the choice is justified.

Practical example: Use deterministic arithmetic for invoice totals and consider bounded AI only for disputed explanations.

Evidence to collect: Intake record, alternatives considered, initial classification and approval pathway

Map · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Measure

Ethics by design

Whose interests, rights and foreseeable harms are considered before design decisions are locked in?

Answer: Identify affected individuals and groups before design decisions are fixed; examine fairness, autonomy, privacy and adverse outcomes, and record difficult tradeoffs for accountable review.

Practical example: Test whether uncommon supplier formats are escalated or rejected unfairly.

Evidence to collect: Stakeholder and harm assessment, tradeoff rationale and ethics review

Map · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Measure · Manage

Third-party risk

Are vendor components, contracts and inherited limitations understood?

Answer: Map supplier dependencies and data transfers, verify relevant vendor claims, document contractual security and change-notification duties, and plan mitigation or exit.

Practical example: Check hosted-model data handling, versions, failure modes and fallback before sharing invoices.

Evidence to collect: Supplier due diligence, model/system documentation and contractual assurance requirements

Map · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Measure

Model / system development and documentation

Are architecture, intended use, assumptions, dependencies and limitations documented?

Answer: Maintain architecture, interfaces, intended uses, assumptions, model/data provenance where available, limitations, evaluation results and versions. Declare missing vendor documentation honestly.

Practical example: Document invoice parser, deterministic rules, AI committee boundaries and the human approval gate.

Evidence to collect: System description, model or system cards, versions and change records

MEASURE · Interpretation, tests and evidence

Evaluate the behavior, limitations and risks identified during Map, with actual measurements and traceable findings.

Measure · PrimaryAlso: Map · Manage

Interpretability

Can relevant users and overseers understand the system's outputs and limitations?

Answer: Provide explanations usable by the person responsible for the decision, and test whether those explanations faithfully reflect verified inputs.

Practical example: Display the applicable contract clause and rate behind a flagged charge, not merely 'the AI thinks this is wrong'.

Evidence to collect: Task-appropriate explanations, factor descriptions and explanation evaluation

Measure · PrimaryAlso: Manage

Testing and validation

Is behavior validated against realistic tasks, affected populations and operating conditions?

Answer: Validate system behavior using representative conditions, independently checked reference results, known adversarial cases and documented uncertainties. Retest changed components.

Practical example: Check rates, totals, missing records and exceptions against approved source documents over varied invoice formats.

Evidence to collect: Validation protocol, performance, robustness and fairness findings with limitations

Measure · PrimaryAlso: Map

Testing types

Are evaluation methods and acceptance criteria chosen for the actual risk?

Answer: Derive test methods from mapped harms: functional correctness, adversarial robustness, security, privacy, safety and fairness or usability where relevant. Predefine criteria and limitations.

Practical example: Test arithmetic correctness, prompt-injection containment, stale contracts and unsupported AI claims separately.

Evidence to collect: Test strategy covering accuracy, adversarial resilience, security and safety where relevant

MANAGE · Release, controls and incident response

Act on the evidence from Map and Measure, assign accountable decisions and sustain safe operation.

Manage · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Measure

Deployment oversight

Are deployment gates, user notices, human oversight and fallback procedures defined?

Answer: Require evidence-based release gates, named authorization, human oversight, user instructions, monitoring and fallback or rollback. Do not infer deployment approval from a build passing.

Practical example: Launch advisory invoice flags only after validation; disputed payment approval stays with a reviewer.

Evidence to collect: Release decision, operating procedures, monitoring and human-control plan

Manage · PrimaryAlso: Govern · Map

Incident management

Can failures or harm be detected, reported, contained, investigated and escalated?

Answer: Establish accessible reporting, severity triage, containment, stakeholder notification, root-cause review and verified corrective action, including regulatory reporting where applicable.

Practical example: An unsupported rate recommendation triggers an incident record, reviewer escalation and regression test before restoration.

Evidence to collect: Incident procedure, severity routing, root-cause review and corrective-action records

04 · Worked illustration

Supplier-invoice validation: when AI helps, and when it should not decide

This is a conceptual assessment example drawn from the site's supplier workflow. It is not a documented approval of a real enterprise deployment.

MAP · Decision boundary

Use ordinary code for totals, schema checks and explicit contract rules. Consider AI only for bounded review of unusual claims with independent evidence and clear human oversight.

MEASURE · Credible failure

Test unauthorized-rate assertions, weak or outdated contract evidence, prompt injection, false escalation and unsupported model claims. Record error rates and denominators, not just anecdotal successes.

MANAGE · Accountable control

Require trusted-source checks, traceable exceptions, vendor controls, a named reviewer for disputed cases, incident escalation and re-evaluation after material changes.

05 · Lifecycle checkpoints

Assessment is a recurring governance activity

Before implementation

Assess strategic fit, alternatives, likely impact and feasibility before committing to AI.

Before a material change

Revisit new models, vendors, data sources, target users, decision authority and deployment contexts.

Throughout operation

Review drift, incidents, control effectiveness, user feedback and evidence at scheduled checkpoints.

When obligations change

Re-evaluate applicable legal, contractual or sector-specific requirements without assuming that NIST alone establishes compliance.
