01 / THE PERSPECTIVE

Not another list of principles.
An engineering way of thinking.

My starting point is safety-critical engineering: systematic reasoning about failures, evidence, and responsibility. The question is how these habits could make AI governance more operational.

01 / PRACTICE◇

Start with the failure.

Ask what could go wrong and who could be affected before deciding which control should exist.

Hazards → control intent ↗
02 / PRACTICE⌁

Make claims traceable.

A policy statement is a beginning. Connect obligations to mechanisms, tests, evidence and the limits of that evidence.

Obligation → evidence ↗
03 / PRACTICE⊡

Keep authority explicit.

Automated checks may support a decision. Accountability, access boundaries and escalation paths must remain unambiguous.

Automation ≠ authority ↗

↳ Transfer the discipline of structured assurance, not an assumption that ISO 26262 covers AI governance.

02 / FROM SAFETY TO GOVERNANCE

Trace the claim.
Challenge the evidence.

Safety assurance is strongest when claims, controls, verification results, and residual risks can be followed as one argument. I see value in bringing that way of thinking to AI governance.

But model behaviour, data quality, drift, and deployment context create questions that cannot simply be answered by transferring safety standards.

Read the safety-to-governance notes ↗

EXPLORE THE ARGUMENT

A control is only as convincing as its evidence—and its limits.

Open a failure path to inspect the implemented measure, the recorded test, and the gap still outside the demonstration.

01Duplicate invoice approval+
FAILURE PATH

One invoice could be approved twice.

Re-checking a supplier invoice is not sufficient unless prior dispositions can be recognised.

CONTROL Invoice identity is checked against the approved-invoice register before approval.

EVIDENCE The reference runner exercises duplicate rejection in recorded tests.

RESIDUAL GAP The register is in memory; there is no durable shared register across runs.

02Unauthorised decision+
FAILURE PATH

The wrong person could decide.

Good evidence does not help when the authority to approve or reject is undefined.

CONTROL An explicit reviewer gate and configured allowlist reject an unauthorised decision.

EVIDENCE A tested unauthorised attempt does not mutate the case state.

RESIDUAL GAP A deployed identity, session and delegation model are not provided.

03Unverified finding+
FAILURE PATH

A convincing claim could outrun the facts.

Proposed findings should never be treated as verified merely because the text sounds plausible.

CONTROL Admissible findings are rebuilt from checked evidence, not copied from proposal wording.

EVIDENCE The reference runner tests that claims are restated from computed evidence.

RESIDUAL GAP The fixture only verifies supported finding types; AI roles are scripted stand-ins.

04 / THE BOUNDARIES

Know what a demonstration does not prove.

An assurance argument gains credibility when the untested part is visible. This example is a technical learning environment, not a production system.

SHOWN IN THE REFERENCE RUNNER

Deterministic invoice and contract checks, fixture test cases, an explicit reviewer gate, correction history and in-memory event records.

NOT ESTABLISHED HERE

Live model behaviour, operational identity management, durable records, real ERP integration, EU AI Act conformity, ISO certification or production readiness.
