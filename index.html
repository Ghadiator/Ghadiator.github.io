Classification before compliance

“High-risk” is a legal conclusion, not a design style.

The intended purpose here is reviewing supplier billing submissions against approved contracts and rate cards. Classification follows the intended purpose, not the architecture — so this showcase asserts no Annex III high-risk classification, and makes no claim of conformity assessment or certification.

Actor analysis

Provider

Develops or has the system developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its name.

Deployer

Who built this, and why

I make consequential AI governable.

My background is functional safety, where assurance is designed into a system rather than documented after it. The same discipline applies to AI that carries consequences: an obligation becomes a type, a config key, a test or a gate — or it does not exist.

The showcase replays runs recorded from the implementation’s test fixtures. Downloadable traces carry their provenance, source references identify the supplier controls, and each mapping states what it does not establish. I would rather show a smaller claim that holds than a larger one that needs the reader not to look closely.
