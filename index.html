Governance as code

Checking a supplier invoice, with the decision left to a person.

Below is what the system is for, what it refuses to do, and where to look. Nothing on this site is a mock-up: every number was produced by executing the reference workflow and exporting the result.
The job
A supplier sends an invoice every month. Someone has to check it against the contract before it is paid — the rates, the totals, the spending limit, the documents that should be attached.
What this is
A working system that does the checking, proposes what looks wrong, and then stops. It never decides. A named reviewer approves, rejects, or asks the supplier to correct and resubmit.
Why it is interesting
Every control is visible, testable and recorded — including the places it is not good enough yet. The hazards it does not fully cover are on the site, not in a drawer.

What it does not do

The limits, in the same place as the claims — not in a footnote.

  • FAILCounts and traces come from the supplier reference runner, not another domain, and measure only its scope.
  • FAILA passing-test count is shown only while its recorded source digest matches.
  • FAILExternal adapters and model proposals are fixtures, not live integrations.
  • FAILIt reaches a validation decision and stops. It never instructs a payment, and no mail leaves the system — the outbox is a fixture.
  • FAILNo live model is called. The proposal adapter is a scripted stand-in; the seam a real one plugs into exists and is configured off.

The four commitments

What every control on this site is in service of.

  • Structure over trustTyped boundaries and explicit allowed transitions.
  • Evidence over assertionFindings must match computed invoice evidence.
  • Humans retain authorityOnly an authorised reviewer decides.
  • Reproducible demonstrationsSynthetic fixture runs produce the displayed traces.

Where to look

15 runs were executed and exported. 14 reached a person; 1 stopped there and was never resumed.

  • Architecture →The system itself. Pick a case and step through it — each component says what it is responsible for.
  • Cases →All fifteen executed runs, with the checks each one failed and the decision each one recorded.
  • Functional safety →What could go wrong, what stops it, and what each control still does not cover.
  • Red team →Adversarial payloads fired at the shipped controls, with the evidence of what happened.
