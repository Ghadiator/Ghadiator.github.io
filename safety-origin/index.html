01 · Transferable engineering habits

A safety discipline can inform governance controls.

02 · Where the analogy stops

AI brings different failure mechanisms.

LIMIT 01

Probabilistic outputs

LLM responses can change across runs; a controlled graph does not turn model content into deterministic facts.

LIMIT 02

Semantic and evidential uncertainty

Claims can be plausible but ungrounded, and reference data can be missing, stale or ambiguous.

LIMIT 03

Adversarial input and authority

Untrusted documents may contain instructions, while models must never acquire approval rights.

LIMIT 04

Ongoing change

Model providers, prompts, data and usage patterns require monitoring, drift testing and controlled updates.

Boundaries of the transfer · original source statements

From functional safety work, I see useful foundations in risk analysis, traceability, verification and accountable release decisions. AI governance also requires its own evaluation of model and data behaviour, bias, misuse and performance after deployment.

This reference model demonstrates selected controls. It carries no automotive safety qualification or AI compliance claim.

Professional safety engineering experience gives this exploration its starting point. These mappings share my reasoning, not equivalence between standards.

DECISION 01Keep deterministic verification behind CoVe reviewsExpand engineering argument ↓
Functional-safety lesson

My functional safety work taught me to separate uncertain behaviour from checks whose results can be reproduced.

How the design explored it

I added CoVe reviews to keep AI-style proposals behind an evidence-verification boundary. In this reference workflow, deterministic checks can reject unsupported findings even if a proposal is wrong; approval still requires the human gate.

DECISION 02Challenge hallucinations with Proponent and OpponentExpand engineering argument ↓
Functional-safety lesson

Architecture and software verification taught me to challenge a proposed result before accepting it.

How the design explored it

I added Proponent and Opponent roles to surface contradictions and challenge hallucinated or unsupported claims. My aim was to improve review quality and confidence in the evidence. Agreement between roles is still not proof of correctness.

Functional-safety lesson

I performed a Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) to examine how this system could fail, the effects of each failure and the controls needed.

How the design explored it

That shaped the failure and edge cases: fabricated findings, missing evidence, incorrect rates and unresolved reviews. The published hazard analysis connects failures to controls, tests and remaining limitations.

Functional-safety lesson

Owning safety cases and supporting assessments taught me to connect each claim to its controls and evidence.

How the design explored it

I added the system card and process audit aspects to make scope, limitations and decisions inspectable. Like monitoring software, the event record and replay dashboard make checks, exceptions and state changes visible and traceable.
