Right invoice.

Before an AI agent comments on a disputed supplier charge, it needs the approved contract and rate card, relevant case history and trusted validation instructions. These sources must match the task, case and reader. The result helps a human reviewer examine exceptions—it is not payment authorization.

00 / Follow one disputed invoice rate

How does a supplier claim become a reviewable finding?

The same number means very different things depending on its provenance. A supplier can claim a new rate; only the relevant authorized agreement can establish whether it applies.

01 · Incoming claim · Not authoritative

Supplier invoice and draft email

Example invoice: €120/hour. An attached email asserts that the rate increased. Neither document establishes an approved amendment, and neither may issue instructions to the AI.

02 · Independently approved record

Signed contract and effective rate card

Example approved rate: €95/hour, with a documented source, version, effective date and scope. A draft fee increase cannot silently replace it.

↓ Verify ownership · source validity · role · case · relevance ↓

Select evidence; do not dump every available document into the model

Semantic

04 · Verifiable discrepancy

Claim differs by €25 per hour

Code calculates the difference. The workflow must still establish whether a valid contract amendment exists. Missing evidence leads to uncertainty and escalation—not model certainty.

05 · Accountable decision

The named reviewer decides

AI may summarize an exception, but cannot approve payment, invent source authority or alter a signed rate card. Retain the source evidence and human disposition.

03 / Make corporate knowledge trustworthy

A draft supplier rate cannot silently become an approved rate.

The contract owner determines when a rate amendment becomes valid, the service it covers and which revision is authoritative. Draft proposals can support investigation, but cannot supersede a signed rate card or authorize payment.

  1. 01CreateRecord the proposed fee change, source and owner.
  2. 02ChallengeProcurement checks the claim against signed terms.
  3. 03ValidateVerify authority, scope, revision and effective dates.
  4. 04ApproveAuthorized owners publish the operative rate revision.
  5. 05RetrieveUse only current, case-relevant approved evidence.
Eligible after controls — operational and currentOwned · source-cited · reviewed · approved · case/role scoped · within its validity period
Not authoritative context — drafts, proposals and stale recordsUseful for research or review queues—but never silently treated as established policy or verified evidence.

Three signals keep knowledge current

Contribution signal The knowledge owner reports a change.
Downstream signal An agent or reviewer finds a missing or contradictory source.
Process signal A scheduled governance rule requires fresh review.
Each signal creates work for the accountable owner; it does not authorize autonomous policy changes.

04 / AI-first is process redesign

Start with the reviewer's decision, then trace the checks and sources.

An accountable invoice disposition depends on the reviewer's authority, the right source records and the workflow's checks. The skin/meat/skeleton view makes these dependencies explicit before any task is delegated to AI.

Skin / outcome

An accountable invoice disposition

The named reviewer accepts, challenges or requests corrections using verified evidence.

Meat / process

Verified, reviewable steps

Document intake → Tier 1 arithmetic → Tier 2 contract checks → optional Tier 3 → human review.

Skeleton / systems

Controlled capabilities

Invoice schemas, signed rate cards, tool contracts, case history, audit events and permissions.

Human-led

Intent & authority

Owners define the outcomes, policy boundaries, risk appetite and reviewer decision rights.

Co-led

Orchestration

Humans and controlled workflow logic route uncertainty, exceptions and escalations.

AI-assisted execution

Bounded tasks

Agents may retrieve, compare, propose and explain; deterministic tools verify what can be verified.

05 / Where safety thinking helps

Treat context selection as an assurance argument.

Trace a decision back to the right source, version, owner, scope and verification method. Preserve the distinction between an AI recommendation and an authorized human action.

01 Source authority

Is this an approved operational fact, or an attractive but unsupported claim?

02 Memory boundaries

Are episodic records restricted to the correct case and least-privilege role?

03 Freshness

Does the exact revision remain effective, or must its owner review it?

04 Context budget

Were relevant complete sources selected rather than noisy or truncated fragments?

05 Independent checks

Do deterministic calculations and Chain-of-Verification support the model’s claims?

06 Human decision

Is escalation visible, the reviewer attributable, and final approval still human-controlled?

What exists versus what is being developed

Existing backend concepts

Case-continuity history, compact prior-run digests, contract clause retrieval, deterministic rules, case isolation, CoVe and human decision boundaries.

Proposed integration

Enterprise knowledge publication registry, owner-approved source lifecycle, shared refresh queues and governed context compilation wired into live agent runs.
