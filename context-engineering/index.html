00 / An example from the actual business workflow

A supplier claims a higher rate. Which source should the AI trust?

The claim in the invoice is not the same as an authorized contract term. The workflow must trace the discrepancy through source authority, case scope and independent checks before any human decision.

01 · Incoming claim · Untrusted

Supplier invoice: €120/hour

An attached email says the rate increased. This document is evidence to examine, not an approved amendment and never a source of AI instructions.

02 · Contract authority · Verified source

Effective rate card: €95/hour

The approved rate card is owned, versioned and checked for applicability. Draft or expired documents cannot silently replace it.

↓ Validate authority · currency · case · role · relevance ↓
03 · Construct minimum sufficient context

Provide only task-relevant, admissible records

Semantic

Signed rate, effective period and evidence reference.

Episodic

Earlier correction from the same invoice case, if authorized.

Procedural

Deterministic checks and human escalation from trusted workflow code.

↓ Deterministic checks first · Optional AI reasoning only for uncertainty ↓
04 · Independent discrepancy finding

€25/hour difference

Code calculates the difference. It does not establish whether a valid amendment exists. Missing evidence triggers an escalation rather than an unsupported model claim.

05 · Human accountability

Reviewer accepts, rejects or requests clarification

An AI explanation does not update contract authority or approve payments. The reviewer evaluates source references and records a traceable disposition.

01 / Distinguish the layers

Which context matters for an invoice review?

Semantic facts, episodic history and procedural rules serve different purposes. Retrieve only verified and authorized material for this invoice, task, role and point in time.

LTM / 01
◇

Semantic memory

What the business has authorized: signed supplier terms, rate cards and their effective dates.

Supplier exampleEffective contractual fee ceiling and approved supplier rate card

Control: source owner, approved version, validity and scope.

LTM / 02
↺

Episodic memory

What happened in this case: prior submissions, disputes, corrections and reviewer decisions.

Supplier exampleThe same case’s earlier correction and reviewer challenge

Control: case isolation; history is not automatically a policy.

How to perform the review: trusted arithmetic, permitted tools, evidence checks and escalation.

Short-term memory / runtime window

Signed rate and contract terms + this case’s correction history + trusted validation rules + permitted tools. Supplier claims remain claims.

03 / Make corporate knowledge trustworthy

A proposed supplier rate is not an authorized rate card.

The contract owner must approve the operative revision, scope and effective dates before a new rate is used in invoice verification. A supplier proposal is not permission to change the checking baseline.

  1. 01CreateAn owner records a candidate claim and original source.
  2. 02ShareExperts challenge details, scope and contradictions.
  3. 03IntegrateCheck permissions, semantics, versions and completeness.
  4. 04PublishHuman approval creates an accountable operational record.
  5. 05UtilizeRetrieve only task-relevant, valid, authorized knowledge.
Eligible after controlsOperational and current

Owned · source-cited · reviewed · approved · case/role scoped · within its validity period

Not authoritative contextDrafts, proposals and stale records

Useful for research or review queues—but never silently treated as established policy or verified evidence.

Three signals keep knowledge current

Contribution signalThe knowledge owner reports a change.

Downstream signalAn agent or reviewer finds a missing or contradictory source.

Process signalA scheduled governance rule requires fresh review.

Each signal creates work for the accountable owner; it does not authorize autonomous policy changes.

04 / AI-first is process redesign

Start with the reviewer’s decision, then trace the checks and sources.

The outcome is not an AI-generated opinion; it is a defensible invoice disposition grounded in approved records and verified controls. Human decision rights stay explicit.

05 / Where safety thinking helps

Treat context selection as an assurance argument.

Trace a decision back to the right source, version, owner, scope and verification method. Preserve the distinction between an AI recommendation and an authorized human action.

01

Source authority

Is this an approved operational fact, or an attractive but unsupported claim?

02

Memory boundaries

Are episodic records restricted to the correct case and least-privilege role?

03

Freshness

Does the exact revision remain effective, or must its owner review it?

04

Context budget

Were relevant complete sources selected rather than noisy or truncated fragments?

05

Independent checks

Do deterministic calculations and Chain-of-Verification support the model’s claims?

06

Human decision

Is escalation visible, the reviewer attributable, and final approval still human-controlled?

What exists versus what is being developed

Existing backend concepts

Case-continuity history, compact prior-run digests, contract clause retrieval, deterministic rules, case isolation, CoVe and human decision boundaries.

Proposed integration
